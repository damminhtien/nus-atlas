Cohesion

Approach 3
Step 1: Pick a notion of cohesion of clusters.
Step 2: Merge clusters whose union is the most cohesive.

Cohesion could mean:
1. Diameter of a cluster = maximum distance between any
two points in the cluster.
2. Average distance between points in the cluster.
3. Density-based cohesion:
•
Take the number of points in the cluster divided by
some measure of volume


Volume could be diameter to the power of dimension of space

BFR Overview

Step 1: Initialize K clusters/centroids
Step 2: Load a small chunk of points from disk to memory
Step 3: For those points that have high probability of
belonging to one of the K clusters, we assign them
accordingly, then summarize and discard them.
Step 4: Run hierarchical clustering on the remaining points,
resulting in some extra mini-clusters, which are also
summarized
Step 5: Try to merge new mini-clusters from step 4 with
existing clusters

Repeat steps 2-5 until all points are examined.

Three Classes of Points

Three classes of points to keep track of:
Discard set (DS) from Step 3
• Points close enough to one of the K centroids to be added
as a member of that cluster.
• They are thus summarized and discarded.
Compression set (CS) from Step 4
• Groups of points that are close to each other, but not
close to any existing centroid.
• These points are summarized as a minicluster, but not
assigned to one of the K big clusters.
Retained set (RS) from Step 4
• Isolated points (singleton clusters from step 4) that are
not summarized yet

Starting CURE

CURE is a two pass algorithm.

Pass 1:
0) Pick a random sample of points that fit in main
memory
1) Initial clusters:
•
Cluster these points hierarchically

E.g. merge clusters based on nearest pair of points
2) Pick representative points:
•
For each cluster, pick some representative points, as
dispersed as possible (like K-means initialization)
•
Move the representative points (say) 20% toward the
centroid of the cluster.

Finishing CURE

After picking representative points, we discard all points of
the cluster, and only describe clusters using these
representatives.

Merge two clusters if they have a pair of representatives
sufficiently close.
• Describe resulting cluster by picking scattered
representatives out of the representatives of its parent
clusters, then shrink towards centroid again.
• We can keep merging until there are no more sufficiently
close clusters.

Summary

Clustering: Given a set of points, with a notion of distance
between points, group the points into some number of
clusters.

Algorithms:
•
Agglomerative hierarchical clustering:

Centroid and clustroid, boundary, cohesion
• K-means:

Initialization, picking K
• BFR: summarize points
• CURE: using representatives at boundary
• For scalable algorithms in the non-Euclidean setting,
see Section 7.5 of the textbook.
